WHAT HAPPENS AT THE COUNCIL AFTER DEATH?

A guide to the Svetlye Sily account of the Council on day forty: the review of one’s life, the figures on the Council, karma, contracts, and free choice.

A recording by Svetlye Sily dated September 6, 2024 describes an energy-information hypnosis session. The interviewer asks about a Council that, according to the participants, the soul encounters forty days after death. The conversation is framed as contact with a “Higher Mind.”

The Council is depicted as a hall of elders. The soul is shown events from earthly life, kind deeds, errors, and choices. Participants describe this as an individual spiritual experience and say the judges can appear differently to each soul.

Who sits on the Council? The recording mentions beings of light and darkness, reptilians, insectoids, and demonic figures. It links their appearance to the soul’s own perception and suggests that elderly, white-bearded faces can sometimes conceal other beings.

Why is a life reviewed? The discussion describes a review of earthly experience and the proposal of a next destination. It also discusses the Wheel of Samsara, repeated incarnation, and karmic tasks.

Can a soul refuse reincarnation? The participants say the soul is given a final word at the end of the Council. They discuss refusing further participation in Samsara, asking for release, and expressing a free choice.

The recording connects freedom from contracts with reflecting on previous actions and rejecting unwanted agreements. It asks whether a soul can reject new obligations and avoid another return to Earth.

The central spiritual theme is personal choice: “I am a free soul. I have the right to choose. I reject all contracts. My choice is God.”

Unresolved earthly relationships may be left behind with forgiveness: “I forgive you completely. We tried in this life. Now you follow your path, and I follow mine.”

The original Svetlye Sily video and full written transcript are linked below. This page summarizes the published session; it does not establish the Council or its alleged participants as verified facts.
